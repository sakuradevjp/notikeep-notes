FAQ

Can NotiKeep see notifications I received before I installed it?

No. NotiKeep can only save notifications that arrive after it's installed and Notification Access is granted. Android doesn't give any app access to past notifications — so please install NotiKeep before you need to recover something. The good news: once it's running, nothing new slips through.

Which Android versions does NotiKeep support?

Android 9.0 (Pie) and newer. Tested on everything from Pixel 4a up through Pixel 9 Pro. Latest Android 15 and 16 are both supported.

How much storage does NotiKeep use?

Text notifications are tiny — megabytes of text covers months of archive. The storage footprint comes from images: each preserved photo is re-encoded to WebP at around 20–80 KB. A heavy user with a thousand image-carrying notifications per month uses roughly 30–80 MB. You can see the exact usage in Settings, and you can clear all images at any time.

What happens to my data if I uninstall?

All NotiKeep data — notifications, images, preferences, bookmarks — is stored in the app's private directory, which Android wipes automatically on uninstall. If you want to keep your archive across a reinstall or a new phone, take an encrypted backup first (Pro feature).

What permissions does NotiKeep need?

Just two: Notification Access (so it can see notifications — every app in this category needs this) and INTERNET (only used by Google Play Billing for Pro purchases). Nothing else — no location, no contacts, no analytics SDKs, no trackers.

Does NotiKeep work with encrypted apps like Signal?

Encrypted apps deliver "New message" placeholder text to Android — there's no preview content for any logger to capture, encrypted or otherwise. NotiKeep saves what the system delivers; if the system gets a placeholder, that's what's archived.

Can I export my data?

Yes. Pro tier creates an encrypted ZIP with the database, all preserved images, your bookmarks, mute lists, and settings. Restore on a new phone in one step. The export goes to a path you pick — no automatic cloud sync.

What happens at the end of the 30-day trial?

You revert to Free. No charge, no auto-conversion. To keep using Pro features, you actively pick a plan from the upgrade screen. We made that the default because nobody likes accidentally getting billed at the end of a trial.

Will it drain my battery or slow my phone?

Android's notification listener is event-driven — there's no polling. NotiKeep does its work only when a notification arrives. Battery impact is negligible in our testing on Pixel 7a and Pixel 9 Pro.

Does NotiKeep upload my notifications anywhere?

No. Everything is stored on-device in NotiKeep's private app directory. Backups go to a path you choose — there is no automatic cloud sync, no "phone home" telemetry, no remote analytics.
